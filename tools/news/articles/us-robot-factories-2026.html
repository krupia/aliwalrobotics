Two kinds of builders

The new US robot plants fall into two groups. Humanoid start-ups like 1X and Figure build their robots in California, starting with small, vertically integrated lines. Established industrial-robot makers FANUC and Yaskawa are building their first large US plants. Both describe these as added capacity, not production moved out of Asia.

What it means for component buyers

  • US demand for joint hardware is rising. Every humanoid has dozens of joints, each with a motor, reducer, encoder and bearings. US assembly at these volumes means more domestic demand for those parts.
  • Vertical integration covers the big parts, not the small ones. 1X winds its own motors and builds batteries, electronics and sensors in-house. Figure builds its own actuators, hands and batteries. Both still buy piece parts such as bearings, magnets, cables and fasteners from outside suppliers.
  • Local plants won't change lead times yet. FANUC's and Yaskawa's US capacity arrives in 2027–28. Until then, servo motors, drives and reducers for these brands keep coming from existing plants overseas.
  • Policy is pushing in the same direction. The 28 October Section 232 proclamation on robotics opens a path to tariffs or domestic-investment incentives if trade talks stall. That adds to the case for US assembly.
