What the summit changed

At President Xi's 23–25 September state visit, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the trade truce would be extended to 10 January 2027. The truce keeps a 20% ceiling on specified tariffs from this administration. It does not cover Section 301 or Section 232 duties, so the total duty on some Chinese goods is higher than 20%.

Other outcomes:

  • Board of Trade. A new US–China Board of Trade recommended more favorable tariff treatment for about $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction. These cuts are not in effect yet.
  • Export controls. China's suspension of certain rare-earth and dual-use export controls runs through 10 January 2027. So does the postponement of the Commerce Department's "Affiliates Rule," which would extend Entity List restrictions to companies 50% or more owned by listed parties.

What USTR did on 6 November

Two China-related measures were due to end at 11:59 pm ET on 9 November. On 6 November USTR posted final notices extending both. As of 8 November, the notices had been posted on USTR's website but not yet published in the Federal Register.

  • The 178 product exclusions. All 178 exclusions are extended, with none added or removed. They cover 164 general exclusions entered under HTS 9903.88.69 and 14 solar-manufacturing exclusions under 9903.88.70. The extension applies to goods entered from 12:01 am ET on 10 November 2026 through 11:59 pm ET on 9 January 2027. Tech Times notes that some text in the notice refers to 10 January; the Dates section and the annexes say 9 January.
  • Port fees. USTR finalized the two-month extension it proposed on 26 October. The Section 301 port fees on China-linked vessels stay suspended through 11:59 pm ET on 9 January 2027.
  • Affiliates Rule. Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security filed a rule on 5 November extending its suspension of the "Affiliates Rule" through 10 January 2027. The rule would extend Entity List and certain other export restrictions to companies 50% or more owned by listed parties. It was scheduled for Federal Register publication on 9 November. Nixon Peabody notes that only the timing changes; the 50% threshold and license requirements are unchanged.

The new end dates line up with the extended truce, which ends on 10 January 2027.

Still pending

A separate Section 301 investigation into structural excess capacity covers 16 economies, not just China. No determination has been published. Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck expects no final implementation until after the November midterms, and any proposed action would go out for public comment first.

What to do now

  • If a part you buy enters under one of the 178 exclusions, keep claiming it under HTS 9903.88.69 or 9903.88.70. Entries after 11:59 pm ET on 9 January 2027 pay the full Section 301 rate unless USTR extends the exclusions again.
  • Plan for another deadline in early January. In the past two rounds, the extension notice came close to, or after, the expiry date.
  • If you export to China, use the time before 10 January to screen customers for 50% ownership by listed parties.
