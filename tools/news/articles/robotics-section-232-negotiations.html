What happened

The proclamation, issued under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, accepts the Commerce Department's finding that robotics, industrial machinery and their parts and components are imported "in such quantities and under such circumstances" that they threaten to impair national security. It covers these goods from any country.

Instead of imposing duties, it directs the Commerce Secretary and the U.S. Trade Representative to pursue or continue negotiations with trading partners. They must report progress within 180 days. If talks don't resolve the concern, the proclamation leaves room for tariffs, quotas, fees or a program to encourage domestic production.

The decision follows a recent pattern. Section 232 cases on wind turbines, commercial aircraft and semiconductors also ended in negotiations, while the cases on cars, drones and polysilicon ended in tariffs.

What it means if you buy robotics components

  • No new duty today. Actuators, reducers, motors, encoders, sensors and controllers pick up no additional Section 232 duty from this action.
  • Existing duties still apply. That includes the normal (MFN) rate, Section 301 duties on Chinese-origin goods and the forced-labor Section 301 tariffs in effect since 24 July.
  • The risk window is spring 2027. The 180-day update lands around late April 2027. For long-lead parts quoted now and delivered after that date, agree in writing who carries any new duty.
