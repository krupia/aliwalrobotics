Assembly is moving faster than the parts

The US is gaining robot assembly lines, but most of what goes into a robot is still imported. WisdomTree's June 2026 review puts the gap in numbers:

  • Industrial robots. The US installed 34,200 industrial robots in 2024, down 9%, against 295,000 in China. It imports most of its industrial robots from Japan and Europe.
  • Humanoids. Chinese vendors shipped nearly 90% of the roughly 13,000 humanoids delivered worldwide in 2025.
  • Components. About 90% of key robotics components are sourced from China. Actuators alone are close to half of a humanoid's bill of materials.

US humanoid makers have answered by building their own critical parts. Figure designs its motors, sensors and electronics and assembles actuators, hands and batteries in-house. Tesla and Apptronik follow the same approach, and Apptronik raised $520 million in February 2026. That covers the high-value subassemblies. The materials and piece parts underneath them, including magnets, bearings, gears and cable sets, still come largely from Asia.

Three pressures on imported parts

  • Tariffs. Chinese-origin parts already pay Section 301 duties plus the 12.5% forced-labor tariff. The 28 October Section 232 finding on robotics leaves room for more duties if talks stall. A first progress report is due around late April 2027.
  • Rare earths. Robot motors depend on rare-earth magnets. China's suspension of certain rare-earth and dual-use export controls runs only to 10 January 2027, the same date the US–China truce ends.
  • Lead times. As US lines ramp, more buyers compete for the same reducers, motors and encoders, and new US capacity from established makers doesn't arrive until 2027–28.

What buyers can do now

  • Qualify a second origin for long-lead joint parts. Reducers, frameless motors and encoders made in Japan, Taiwan or Germany often land on a lower combined tariff than Chinese-origin parts, which can offset a higher unit price.
  • Buffer magnet-dependent parts such as motors, actuators and encoders ahead of the 10 January 2027 rare-earth date.
  • Record origin and manufacturer for every subcomponent. The same records support tariff classification, UFLPA screening and any future Section 232 exclusion request.
