The UFLPA import-ban list reaches 190 companies

Goods made by companies on the UFLPA Entity List are presumed to involve forced labor and are barred from entering the US. The 43 additions in August were the largest expansion so far and included electronics and aluminum suppliers.

Published 29 Oct 2026 · Aliwal Robotics

Key dates

  • 31 Jul 2026DHS adds 43 entities, the largest expansion to date (published 3 Aug 2026).
  • 29 Oct 2026Update adds three entities. The list now has 190.

What the list does

Under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, CBP presumes that goods mined, produced or manufactured wholly or in part by a listed company were made with forced labor. That bars their import under 19 U.S.C. 1307 unless the importer rebuts the presumption. The ban follows the goods through the supply chain: a finished product can be detained because a listed company made one of its inputs.

What changed this year

  • 31 July 2026: DHS added 43 entities, published in the Federal Register on 3 August (91 FR 48913). Covington called it the largest single expansion since the law took effect. The priority sectors were aluminum, apparel, cotton and tomatoes. The additions also reached mining, pharmaceuticals, electronics, energy and transportation infrastructure.
  • 29 October 2026: A further update added BGI Genomics, MGI Tech and Shenzhen BGI Technology, bringing the list to 190 entities.

Since the law took effect, CBP has detained shipments worth nearly $4 billion. Its dashboard shows detention activity fell after January 2025. Covington reads the August expansion as a possible sign of renewed enforcement.

What it means for components and devices

  • Aluminum housings, cable assemblies, batteries and electronic sub-assemblies all have multi-tier supply chains where a listed company can appear.
  • Ask suppliers for a bill of materials with origin and manufacturer for each major input, and check names against the current list before you place a purchase order, not after the goods ship.
  • If CBP detains a shipment, rebutting the presumption takes detailed tracing documents. Collecting them before shipping is much faster than after a detention.

We screen supplier names and major sub-component makers against the current UFLPA Entity List before confirming an order.
