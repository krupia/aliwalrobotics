The US–China truce runs to 10 January, but two 9 November deadlines still need paperwork

The truce was extended at the September summit. The 178 Section 301 product exclusions and the suspended port fees each need their own USTR notice, and so far only the port fees have one in progress.

Published 29 Oct 2026 · Aliwal Robotics

Key dates

  • 23–25 Sep 2026Xi state visit to Washington. Truce extended to 10 Jan 2027.
  • 26 Oct 2026USTR proposes extending the port-fee suspension to 9 Jan 2027. Comments due 30 Oct.
  • 9 Nov 2026178 Section 301 exclusions and the current port-fee suspension end at 11:59 pm ET unless extended.
  • 10 Jan 2027Extended truce ends.

What the summit changed

At President Xi's 23–25 September state visit, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the trade truce would be extended to 10 January 2027. The truce keeps a 20% ceiling on specified tariffs from this administration. It does not cover Section 301 or Section 232 duties, so the total duty on some Chinese goods is higher than 20%.

Other outcomes:

  • Board of Trade. A new US–China Board of Trade recommended more favorable tariff treatment for about $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction. These cuts are not in effect yet.
  • Export controls. China's suspension of certain rare-earth and dual-use export controls runs through 10 January 2027. So does the postponement of the Commerce Department's "Affiliates Rule," which would extend Entity List restrictions to companies 50% or more owned by listed parties.

Why 9 November still matters

A verbal extension does not change a Federal Register deadline. Two China-related measures are tied to 9 November:

  • Port fees. On 26 October USTR proposed suspending the Section 301 port fees on China-linked vessels, and the related duties in its Annex V.A, through 9 January 2027. Public comments close 30 October.
  • The 178 product exclusions. These exclusions, entered under HTS 9903.88.69 and 9903.88.70, let certain Chinese goods avoid Section 301 duties. They expire at 11:59 pm ET on 9 November. Trade press reported no extension notice for them as of early October. When the previous round lapsed on 29 November 2025, USTR's extension was not published until 1 December.

Still pending

A separate Section 301 investigation into structural excess capacity covers 16 economies, not just China. No determination has been published. Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck expects no final implementation until after the November midterms, and any proposed action would go out for public comment first.

What to do now

  • If any part you buy enters under one of the 178 exclusions, budget for the full Section 301 rate on entries from 10 November until a new notice is published.
  • Where you can, move shipments of excluded parts so they enter before 10 November.
