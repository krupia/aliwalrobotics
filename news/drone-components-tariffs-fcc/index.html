Drone tariffs and the FCC Covered List also reach parts that robots use

The FCC now treats foreign-made drone cameras, sensors, batteries, motors, flight controllers and radios as critical components. Separate Section 232 drone duties of up to 100% started on 3 September.

Published 29 Oct 2026 · Aliwal Robotics

Key dates

  • 22 Dec 2025FCC adds foreign-produced drones and critical components to the Covered List.
  • 7 Jan 2026Blue UAS items exempted. Domestic end products exempted until 1 Jan 2027.
  • 13 Aug 2026Section 232 drone tariff proclamation.
  • 3 Sep 2026Most drone tariffs take effect.
  • 1 Jan 2027Domestic-end-product exemption from the Covered List ends unless renewed.
  • 9 Feb 2027Delayed tranche of drone tariffs begins.

Two separate measures

FCC Covered List (equipment authorization). Since 22 December 2025, foreign-produced uncrewed aircraft systems and their critical components have been on the FCC Covered List. The FCC won't issue new equipment authorizations for them. Models authorized before then can still be imported, sold and used. The FCC's list of critical components "includes, but is not limited to":

  • data transmission and communications systems
  • flight controllers
  • ground control stations and controllers
  • navigation systems
  • sensors and cameras
  • batteries and battery management systems
  • motors

Items on the Defense Department's Blue UAS list are exempt. Items that qualify as "domestic end products" are exempt until 1 January 2027.

Section 232 tariffs (customs duty). A proclamation on 13 August 2026 set duties on drones and drone components. Most took effect on 3 September, and some are delayed until 9 February 2027.

RateApplies to
100%Drones over 25 kg maximum takeoff weight, drones with thermal imaging, docking stations and certain critical components
25%Smaller drones without specified sensitive capabilities, and other drone components
15%Drones and components from the EU, Japan, Liechtenstein, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan
10%Drones from the UK

The 15% and 10% rates apply only if substantially all the hardware, software and technology comes from the listed countries and the US.

Why robotics and data-capture buyers should care

Many of the parts named for drones are also used in robots and capture rigs: cameras, IMUs and navigation modules, radios, battery packs and brushless motors. A part's tariff treatment follows its HTS classification and the proclamation's annexes, not just what it's used for. A component that's classified as drone-specific can carry a duty a general-purpose equivalent doesn't.

Before quoting cameras, radios, batteries or motors, we check each part number against the drone annexes and its FCC authorization status.
